# Forever's own client data outranks every compilation

The most useful recent Forever data for Krool World Forever is Forever's own client tables on wago.tools: product `wowf`, **build 1.60.1.70888 posted 2026-11-10**. Blizzard's Forever hotfix and development-note posts come second, and the two runtime sources the bundle already reads, QuestieDB and Auctionator, come next. Crowd-measured loot data now exists and grows daily. foreverdroprate.com had **10,693 corpses** on Nov 10, and the Forever Drops addon and ForeverDB collect more. None of these publishes a license or an export, so they can check Kill Value but cannot ship inside it. The assumption that cmangos 1.12 data is accurate fails in specific, measurable places. Forever changed quality on **169** and item level on **265** of the 14,251 item ids it shares with Classic Era. It added **four disenchant brackets** for low-level blue items, reworked dungeon loot, and added zones and mobs cmangos has never seen. Disenchant Value is the most exposed feature, because it pins 7,994 classic items to their 1.12 tables whatever Forever now says about their quality or level. Of the 3,131 pinned items visible in Forever's client table, **8 went from green to blue and 51 changed item level**; the other 4,863 are hidden from client data, so their changes are unknown. Kill Value's vendor prices are mostly safe, since it reads the client's sell price first. No source has refuted open-world drop rates for unchanged classic mobs, and one spot check below matches the 1.12 rate.

First-party client data and Blizzard's own notes win because the bundle can derive tables from them, as it already does for Skill-Up Odds. Crowd sites rank lower despite real measurements, because they offer no license, no export and no named operator.

### Client tables and Blizzard notes are the only sources the generators should build on

wago.tools serves Forever as its own product, and its CSV exports work from PowerShell with a browser User-Agent, though the HTML pages return 403 ([wago.tools builds API](https://wago.tools/api/builds)). A diff of 70888 against Classic Era 1.15.9.70576 shows how far Forever has moved. SkillLineAbility has **7,853 rows against 6,143**, and most old Blacksmithing, Leatherworking and Tailoring recipes have yellow and grey thresholds **about 25 points lower**. For example, Blacksmithing 2664 moved from 115/140 to 90/115 ([SkillLineAbility, wowf](https://wago.tools/db2/SkillLineAbility/csv?build=1.60.1.70888)). This confirms Skill-Up Odds is on the right source. Classic thresholds would be wrong for most of the recipes the panel ranks. The same exports carry two tables the bundle does not read yet: ItemSparse, with Forever's quality, item level and vendor price for every client item ([ItemSparse, wowf](https://wago.tools/db2/ItemSparse/csv?build=1.60.1.70888)), and ItemDisenchantLoot, with the disenchant brackets ([ItemDisenchantLoot, wowf](https://wago.tools/db2/ItemDisenchantLoot/csv?build=1.60.1.70888)). Both feed the fixes in the contradictions section below.

There are two caveats, and both are unresolved. First, nobody has checked whether the CSVs include Blizzard's server hotfixes. Mild Spices shows the gap: the Nov 5 hotfix says it now sells for 1 copper (was 2), while the ItemSparse diff shows Era 0 and Forever 1 ([Blizzard hotfixes](https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/en-us/news/24315281)). Second, the **Nov 6 Tanning Rack change** (requirement 140 to 125, skill-ups reduced by 15) may not be in the shipped rows that `gen-skillup.js` reads. ForeverChanges fills this gap by reading hotfixes from player game caches collected by Raidbots ([ForeverChanges: About](https://foreverchanges.pro/about)), which makes its undocumented-hotfix tracker the natural trigger to regenerate. Build 70888 also landed in wow-ui-source on Nov 10 with one generated-API change, an `isCrossShard` field on party-invite info ([Gethe/wow-ui-source forever](https://github.com/Gethe/wow-ui-source/tree/forever)). The repo's "live is 70862" note is now stale if 70888 is live, and both the allowlist and the Skill-Up thresholds need a regenerate-and-diff. wago.tools' own terms of use were not checked.

Blizzard's posts are the authoritative list of what changed on the server, where client data cannot see. The Nov 9 hotfixes raised **dungeon enemy XP by 50%**. They gave loot to bosses that dropped none (Zarla Ober; Vitrix, Snowtalon and Cinder), made Heart of the Mountain lootable more than once, and let Waylaid Crates and Craftsman's Writs drop in dungeons. The Nov 6 hotfixes corrected loot for Ridgeshade Lurkers and Krek the Noxious ([Blizzard hotfixes](https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/en-us/news/24315281); [forum mirror](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/wow-forever-hotfixes-november-9-2026/2403040/5)). The development notes add **"many profession-created items had their vendor prices reduced"** (Oct 16), common and uncommon wands selling for 1 copper (Oct 1), and some Blacksmithing and Leatherworking recipes moving to dungeon drops (Oct 1) ([Beta Development Notes](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/2360696)). Each entry names a mob or item whose 1.12 numbers are now wrong.

## Measured loot exists, but nothing licensed can ship in Kill Value

foreverdroprate.com is the strongest Forever loot data set found. Players run the Blackthornes Farmer addon, which records "every corpse looted and what was in it, every herb, ore and catch". The site merges uploads without double counting, shows the corpse count beside every rate, marks rates under 20 corpses "few data", and draws a 95% band. It is growing fast: an earlier snapshot the same day showed 7,318 corpses, and by the later fetch it had **10,693 corpses across 984 mobs** ([Forever Drop Rate](https://foreverdroprate.com/); [Blackthornes Farmer](https://www.curseforge.com/wow/addons/blackthornes-farmer)). It gives no download, API, terms or operator name, and helper accounts are invite-only. The Forever Drops addon also records mob, gathering, pickpocket and chest loot, with the profession skill at loot time, and spreads data over addon channels. Its license is **All Rights Reserved**, and it says "data that doesn't add up is thrown away" without saying how ([Forever Drops](https://www.curseforge.com/wow/addons/forever-drops)). ForeverDB's companion addon uploads dungeon boss loot per kill, vendor contents and AH prices, again with no license for ForeverDB's own data ([ForeverDB addon](https://foreverdb.net/addon)). None of the three can be copied into `Data.lua`. Using them would need the operator's permission, and foreverdroprate's operator is reachable only through the Blackthornes Discord.

A spot check against the bundle's own baseline shows what these sites are good for. foreverdroprate reports Linen Cloth from Razormane Water Seeker at **48.1% over 27 corpses** ([Forever Drop Rate](https://foreverdroprate.com/)). The cmangos dump in `tools/killvalue-data/sqlcache/ClassicDB.sql` has NPC 3267 dropping Linen Cloth at **38.5%** in stacks of 1 to 3 (0.77 per kill in `KillValue/Data.lua`). That falls inside the 95% band for 13 of 27, roughly 31% to 66%. This open-world classic mob shows no contradiction yet, but the sample is far too small to show one. Two other sample mobs on the site, Al'Aketh Stormchaser and Hippogryph Protector, do not appear in the cmangos dump at all. The site is strongest exactly where Kill Value has no baseline: Forever-only mobs, and Zephras Isle, its largest zone with 1,366 corpses. A personal drop counter like DropAtlas is not a new idea for the bundle: Kill Value already has one. Its learned layer blends observed per-NPC rates with the baseline (K = 10 pseudo-kills) and goes observed-only after 3 corpses for creatures with no baseline (`docs/features/killvalue.md`). The honest use of foreverdroprate is as a manual check for the generator and the docs.

Two other loot claims weaken the per-player math rather than the tables. Blizzard's Josh "Aggrend" Greenfield said quest drops are now spread round-robin among grouped players, which players have since noticed ([Warcraft Tavern](https://www.warcrafttavern.com/forever/news/quest-drops-distribute-more-evenly-in-wow-forever/); [Icy Veins](https://www.icy-veins.com/wow-forever/news/players-have-felt-a-change-to-group-quest-drops-in-wow-forever/)). Kill Value's learned quest-item rates from group play therefore measure the group's share, not independent rolls. Forum players report quest-item drops feeling low, for example 15 murlocs for one Longshore Murloc Gill, with no Blizzard reply and no comparison against Classic ([Blizzard forum](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/quest-item-drop-rate-too-lowwww/2404646)). These are anecdotes, not rates. Wowhead's Forever drop percentages rest on samples of up to about 290,000 kills per mob, more than five days of Forever could produce, so they are probably Classic data carried over. That claim comes from a gold-selling site ([timesaver.gg](https://timesaver.gg/blog/wow-forever-hyperspawn-grind-spots)), but the arithmetic holds. Do not treat Wowhead percentages as Forever truth.

## Where cmangos 1.12 breaks, and where it still holds

The evidence against 1.12 is strong for item identity and new content, and absent for open-world drop rates of unchanged mobs. The table lists each contradiction with how hard it hits the bundle as the code stands.

| 1.12 assumption | Forever evidence | Bundle exposure |
|---|---|---|
| A classic item keeps its 1.12 quality and item level | 169 quality and 265 item-level changes among 14,251 shared ids ([ItemSparse](https://wago.tools/db2/ItemSparse/csv?build=1.60.1.70888)); 1,417 of 4,041 revealed classic items changed stats, effects or damage ([ForeverChanges](https://foreverchanges.pro/hidden-items)) | **Medium, partly unknown.** Disenchant Value's `Data.items` pins 7,994 items to their 1.12 table; 59 of the 3,131 visible in Forever's client changed, 4,863 are hidden; Least Valuable inherits it |
| Low-level blue disenchants follow 1.12 bands | Four new rare brackets (armor and weapon, item level 5-15 and 16-20); the rare 20-25 row starts at 21 ([ItemDisenchantLoot](https://wago.tools/db2/ItemDisenchantLoot/csv?build=1.60.1.70888)); dungeon boss uniques raised to rare ([Massively OP](https://massivelyop.com/2026/09/13/blizzcon-2026-world-of-warcraft-forevers-deep-dive-panel-talks-group-play-progression-and-item-updates/)) | **Medium-high.** The bundle maps every blue at item level 25 or below to Small Glimmering Shard; Forever's yields are server-side and unknown |
| Creature loot matches 1.12 | Reworked dungeon loot, recipes moved to dungeon drops, corrected loot on Forever mobs, new zones and dungeons ([hotfixes](https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/en-us/news/24315281); [dev notes](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/2360696)) | **Medium** in the open world, where new mobs have no baseline and the learned layer covers them; **none** inside instances, where Kill Value looks nothing up |
| Drops roll independently per player | Round-robin quest drops in groups ([Warcraft Tavern](https://www.warcrafttavern.com/forever/news/quest-drops-distribute-more-evenly-in-wow-forever/)) | **Low-medium.** Skews learned quest-item rates from grouped play |
| Vendor prices match 1.12 | 148 sell and 219 buy changes; wands, profession goods and Mild Spices repriced ([dev notes](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/2360696)) | **Low.** Kill Value and Disenchant read `C_Item.GetItemInfo` first; the 1.12 `Data.sell` serves only uncached items |
| Sayge's gate is the fortune buff (cmangos) | Client 4 h death-proof marker; owner's online-time measurement | **None.** Fortune already models it |
| 1.12 respawn timers | Level-banded launch respawns, hotfixed per camp ([timesaver.gg quoting Blizzard](https://timesaver.gg/blog/wow-forever-hyperspawn-grind-spots)) | **None today.** No feature uses respawns |

The Disenchant pin is the finding to act on first, because the fix is cheap and the error is certain wherever it applies. `Features/Disenchant/Disenchant.lua` checks `D.items[itemId]` before it looks at quality or item level, and the feature doc presents this as a strength: "classic items stay exact even if Forever reports other item levels" (`docs/features/disenchant.md`). A check made for this report measured how far that premise fails. It compared the pinned ids against the 70888 and Era 1.15.9.70576 ItemSparse exports ([wowf](https://wago.tools/db2/ItemSparse/csv?build=1.60.1.70888), [era](https://wago.tools/db2/ItemSparse/csv?build=1.15.9.70576)), with Era standing in for 1.12 because wago.tools serves no 1.12 client. Only **3,131 of the 7,994 pinned items** appear in Forever's client table at all. Of those, **8 went from green to blue**, all with unchanged item level: Cloak of Blight, Orb and Staff of Soran'ruk, Cragwood Maul, Conservator Helm, Shieldplate Sabatons, Windshear Leggings and Bloodsail Admiral's Hat. **51 changed item level**. At least 13 of the changed items would get a different table if derived from Forever's own values. Cloak of Blight, for example, is pinned to the green table (Vision Dust and Lesser Nether Essence), while a blue at its level falls in the Small Radiant Shard band. The other **4,863 pinned items are missing from Forever's client table**, consistent with Blizzard hiding item data until an item drops on the server ([Method.gg](https://www.method.gg/wow-forever/no-ptrs-and-new-datamining-restrictions-coming-to-world-of-warcraft-forever)), so no export can show whether they changed. ForeverChanges' tracker, which has revealed 4,041 hidden classic items, puts the changed share at 1,417 ([ForeverChanges](https://foreverchanges.pro/hidden-items)). That is about a third, though it counts stat changes too, not only quality and level. The dungeon uniques Blizzard raised to rare most likely sit in this hidden set. The fix comes in two parts. At runtime, Disenchant already reads Forever's quality from `GetItemInfo`, and each pinned entry implies a quality (entries 1-31 green, 41-50 blue, 61-65 epic), so it can drop the pin whenever the two disagree; this also covers hidden items once the client has seen them. In the generator, `gen-disenchant.js` can drop pins where Forever's ItemSparse shows a different item level. The four new brackets still have unknown yields. Until someone measures them, the tooltip should mark blue items of item level 5 to 20 as unverified rather than show Small Glimmering Shard with false confidence.

The vendor-price risk is smaller than the 1.12 assumption suggests, because the code already guards it. `ItemFacts` in `KillValue/Core.lua` returns the client's sell price whenever the item is cached, and Disenchant Value and Least Valuable read `GetItemInfo` directly. Blizzard's repricings therefore reach the tooltips without a data rebuild, as long as hotfixed prices reach the client item cache. What 1.12 still supplies, and what no source refutes, is the open-world loot table for classic mobs Forever left alone. No published comparison exists for any such mob. The one check above agrees with cmangos, so the baseline holds as a prior and the learned layer is the right correction.

## Smaller wins for the other features, and five features the data suggests

**Rare Alert** detects rares from `UnitClassification`, not from an id list, so a rare list does not improve detection. ForeverDB's /rares page still adds what the alert lacks: **357 rares**, each tagged "seen in Forever" (65) or "Classic data", with respawn ranges, spawn maps and last sightings, extracted from client 70862 on Nov 9 ([ForeverDB rares](https://foreverdb.net/rares)). Hannah Bladeleaf's roughly 7-minute respawn, tagged "seen in Forever", hints that some timers differ from Classic, but the method is unstated. Since there is no license, the shippable route is QuestieDB's NPC ranks, added Nov 2 ([Questie releases](https://github.com/Questie/Questie/releases)). The bundle already reads QuestieDB at runtime, so a "rares in this zone" list costs no licensing question. Forever-only rares such as Nightscreech in Teldrassil ([Blizzard forum](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/new-rare-spawn-white-owl-in-teldrassil/2355766)) and Petopia's Forever rare pets list ([Petopia](https://www.wow-petopia.com/forever/browse.php?id=rare)) are the test cases for checking that QuestieDB covers them.

**Camp Guide** covers campfires, not grinding camps, so the grind-spot and respawn compilations do not feed it. ForeverDB's camping page, extracted from client 70862, confirms Camp Guide's model: camp features need a minute of sitting or crafting nearby, all camping features share a 1-hour cooldown, and the Camp Tent raises rested XP to 5% of a level ([ForeverDB camping](https://foreverdb.net/camping)). Warcraft Tavern says campfires last 10 minutes ([Warcraft Tavern camping](https://www.warcrafttavern.com/forever/guides/camping/), from a search snippet), which is shorter than Camp Guide's 15-minute expiry for the hovered list.

**Sayge's Fortune** needs no new data. The owner's measurement stands against warcraft.wiki.gg, whose "2 hours (real time)" entry, edited 2026-07-25, Forever contradicts ([warcraft.wiki.gg](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Sayge%27s_Fortunes)). It agrees with Icy Veins' Classic guide, which counts the 4-hour cooldown "only from time you are logged in" ([Icy Veins](https://www.icy-veins.com/wow-classic/darkmoon-faire-guide-for-wow-classic), from a search snippet). The faire runs in Elwynn from Nov 9 and moves to Mulgore on Dec 7 ([Warcraft Tavern](https://www.warcrafttavern.com/forever/news/the-darkmoon-faire-is-now-open-in-wow-forever/)). The "Sayge's Dark Fortune" names in Forever's 1304767-1304782 block do not mark a new, darker variant. The classic fortunes 23735-23738 carry the same name (`docs/08-darkmoon-faire.md`), and `Fortune.lua` already treats both blocks as fortunes.

**Skill-Up Odds** has no community data to replace the owner's **174 measured crafts**. No public set measures Forever's yellow or green chances. The Metabot calculator assumes the same linear rule the owner fitted ([Metabot](https://metabot.gg/en/wow-forever/profession-leveling-calculator)), which corroborates the model without measuring it. A boost site says the Legacy perk Working Overtime adds **4 percentage points per rank, 20 at max** ([Boostroom](https://boostroom.com/blog/wow-forever-journeyman-professions-guide-leveling-75150-recipes-trainers-and-materials)). The repo's own doc records guides split between "raises the chance" and "adds a second point" (`docs/features/skillup.md`). The spell's effects in wago.tools SpellEffect would settle it. Some new recipes have grey thresholds above 300 (Leatherworking 1254967-1254970 at 320/340), so either the cap is not 300 or those recipes stay orange to the cap.

**Least Valuable** and the other price readers gain most from Auctionator calls the bundle does not use yet: `IsAuctionDataExactByItemLink` (flags a random-suffix green priced from its base item), `GetDisenchantPriceByItemID` (a cross-check on Disenchant Value) and `GetVendorPriceByItemID` (items the client has not cached). One caveat matters for every tooltip. On Forever, Auctionator keys its whole price database by ruleset name alone (the owner's live file holds a single `"PvE"` entry). Alliance, Horde and Blackwater house scans therefore merge, and the honest label is "last scanned price on your ruleset" (local `Auctionator/Source_ModernAH/Variables/Main.lua`). Blizzard's own Forever auction API cannot seed shipped prices: its terms impose a **30-day retention cap** and forbid transferring the data to third parties ([Blizzard Developer API Terms](https://www.blizzard.com/en-us/legal/a2989b50-5f16-43b1-abec-2ae17cc09dd6/blizzard-developer-api-terms-of-use)). It also returns identical houses for every ruleset ([API forum thread](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/blizzard/t/wow-forever-api-what-works-so-far-plus-a-ts-client/60334)). TSM 4.15 claims Forever support from launch day, but it names no Forever price sources ([TSM news](https://tradeskillmaster.com/news/tsm-4-15-brings-wow-forever-support-plus-new-desktop-app-1180)), so it stays a later, optional second source. **Quest Trash** keeps using QuestieDB, which updated again on Nov 10. **Setups** draws on none of these compilations. What matters to it is that 70425 reskinned the unit frames and 70888 edited the target frame and nameplates ([warcraft.wiki.gg 1.60.1 notes](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Patch_1.60.1/API_changes/notes); [wow-ui-source](https://github.com/Gethe/wow-ui-source/tree/forever)), so any layout anchored to Blizzard frames needs a recheck each build.

The data also points to five advice-only features, all built on the player's own observations so no third-party terms apply. The first is an **instance-entry counter**. Guides agree on 5 entries per hour but cite no Blizzard source, while the Mainline client allows 10 on retail ([Fextralife](https://wowforever.wiki.fextralife.com/Dungeons); [Blizzard forum](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/you-have-entered-too-many-instances-recently/1778568)). The counter should learn the cap from the first "too many instances" error rather than hard-code it. The second is a **raid lockout reader**: Forever's raids open Dec 9 with a planned weekly reset for Onyxia too, where Classic resets her every 5 days ([Icy Veins raid preview](https://www.icy-veins.com/wow-forever/news/weekly-reset-attunement-locations-raid-sizes-official-wow-forever-raid-preview/)). It should read saved instances from the client, not compute Classic cycles. The third is a **session readout** of kills, XP per hour and Kill Value per hour, which would replace the anecdotal gold-per-hour guide figures; those figures change with every respawn hotfix anyway. The fourth is a **node and pool value tooltip** for Forever-only herbs such as Marefoil, Demonsage and Death Lotus, whose locations guides still list as unknown ([The Forever Era gathering](https://theforeverera.com/en/gathering/)). The fifth is a **learned disenchant layer** that records the player's own disenchant results. That is the only way the bundle will learn what the four new brackets actually yield.

## Conclusion

The bundle's real exposure is not stale prices, which the code already reads from the client, but **tables keyed by item id that assume an id still means its 1.12 item**. Forever kept the ids and changed the items: quality, level, stats and disenchant brackets. Any generator that joins on id alone inherits that drift. The durable fix is to treat cmangos as a prior keyed by id *and* Forever's current item facts, diff each new `wowf` build's ItemSparse and ItemDisenchantLoot, and rebuild whenever ForeverChanges' hotfix tracker shows an item change. Builds are landing every few days (70840, 70862 and 70888 within four days), so this has to run every build.

Licensing makes the community's best measurements reference material, not ingredients. The sources that are both Forever-measured and shippable are the ones the bundle creates itself. Kill Value's learned layer and the Skill-Up craft log already hold data nobody else publishes, and no public source has anything like the owner's 174 measured crafts on Forever's skill-up curve. An opt-in export of those learned tables, under the owner's own license, would make the bundle a Forever data source rather than a consumer of borrowed Classic data. The permission ask worth making is to foreverdroprate's operator, whose per-mob rates with confidence bands would make the strongest validation set for Kill Value's Forever-only mobs.
